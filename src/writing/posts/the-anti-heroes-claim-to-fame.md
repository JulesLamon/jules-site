From Superman to Wonder Woman the screens of televisions have been littered with unrealistic characters. Heroes that are better than the average human morally, mentally, and physically have been a staple point for modern media. Though these qualities might seem like a strength they were ultimately the downfall of the traditional hero. People looked to these heroes for hope. For a feeling of safety and trust that they could not find in the ordinary world. However, the reality finally set in that these heroes could not offer any protection. That they were merely a fantasy and no one like them would ever exist. The hope they once offered was lost. Yet, this could not be the end as Russian novelist, Fyodor Dostoyevsky once said, “To live without hope is to cease to live.” So, humanity found its new hope. In the messiness and flaws of heroes like Batman, Green Arrow, and Iron man a new hope was born. These flawed and imperfect new heroes received the name the anti-hero. Humanity found in them the hope it needed. Hope that is based on similarity to the characters, empathizing with the character, and even illogically forgiving the character. The anti-hero did not offer the false hope of a fake savior instead it offered hope in humanity.

An anti-hero is “A central character in a story, movie, or drama who lacks conventional heroic attributes” Anti-heroes are imperfect. They have flaws and not-so-perfect morals, but they still manage to do the right thing. When Stan Lee the creator of Marvel was describing his favorite superhero, he said this “He’s the one who’s most like me- nothing ever turns out 100% okay; he’s got a lot of problems, and he does things wrong, and I can relate to that.” People’s favorite heroes are often the ones that they believe are most like them. The fact remains that no one’s perfect, and people’s weaknesses aren’t usually as flashy as kryptonite. In the anti-hero, people have someone flawed in ways that are identifiable – whether family problems, loose morals, or bad habits – but they rise above these flaws in a heroic manner. That gives hope that perhaps anyone can be a hero too. Perhaps people’s flaws will not stop them from helping the way all those anti-heroes on the silver screen help.

There comes a certain point in the story arc of the anti-hero that you begin to empathize with this character. The viewer begins to understand the struggles that the hero faces and perhaps even relate them back to the real world. Sometimes this can be related to family problems, societal struggles, or identity issues. The anti-hero can face so many issues and the viewer can almost always see these same problems in their own life. As Pakistani novelist, Mohsin Hamid puts it “Empathy is about finding echoes of another person in yourself.” The more you understand the struggles of the anti-hero the more you will empathize with the anti-hero. When you emphasize with someone you want them to succeed. You want them to be happy and ultimately win. It is with this team spirit that the viewer finds hope for the anti-hero, and in turn, hope for themselves. When you relate so much to a character you begin to see yourself as that character. Their wins can be your wins and it is with this promising thought that the popularity of the anti-hero will continue to rise.

It’s no secret that sometimes anti-heroes do questionable things. The anti-hero has been known to resort to torture, killing, and stealing to achieve the necessary results. In real life, people would most likely crucify the anti-hero for doing such things. Yet, when they are on the silver screen they are safe from reproach. Isn’t this quite a hypocritical approach? Why are people so willing to forgive when the character is fictional? Well, the reason is actually quite illogical. It is through the actor-observer bias of the mind that the anti-hero can continue to flourish. The actor-observer bias is the tendency of the mind to attribute others’ actions to their personality while attributing its own actions to external factors. For example, if you were to come upon someone shooting a man what would be your initial thought? Probably that this is a dangerous person and you need to get away and call the police to come to protect you. Now switch to the mind of the person that just shot a man they probably know it was self-defense. They probably think they had to do it and are still a fairly harmless person that was just forced into this decision. Based on the available knowledge reactions to a situation can be polar opposites. As you watch the story arc of an anti-hero you have already begun to empathize with them. You have already begun drawing similarities between them and yourself and you want them to be good. As psychologist Travis Langley puts it "The way our brain is made up means that if you’re with people, they’re part of your group and you want to stay with them and see good in them because it actually helps us to survive." Even the fallacy of the human mind can be an aid to the plight of the anti-hero. After all, humans need hope.

The survival instinct of the human mind is remarkable. People needed hope to keep them going and the anti-hero was the solution. Though this new rise of the anti-hero may not be appreciated by all it was a necessary part of survival. Now those that had lost faith in themselves or in humanity as a whole can find it again in all the anti-hero represents. Each connection made with the antihero whether it be similarity in characteristics, empathy, or faulty forgiveness, can bring you closer to hope. After all “The miserable have no other medicine, but only hope.”
